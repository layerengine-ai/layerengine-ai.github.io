Recruiting Strategy

90-Day Real Estate Brokerage Recruiting Plan for Consistent Agent Growth

September 11, 2026 8 min read LayerEngine.ai

Most brokerages do not have a recruiting problem for one dramatic reason. They have a consistency problem. Recruiting starts when a leader feels pressure, slows down when production improves, and disappears when the team is busy. That creates unpredictable growth and forces owners to rely on personal relationships, old lists, or last-minute outreach.

A 90-day real estate brokerage recruiting plan replaces that cycle with a focused operating rhythm. It gives brokerage owners and mortgage team leaders a clear producer offer, a defined audience, a repeatable follow-up path, and a scorecard that shows where conversations are getting stuck.

90days to install a repeatable recruiting rhythm
3phases from positioning to conversion
5core metrics for weekly review

These are planning targets for system design, not industry-wide performance benchmarks.

Start with the offer, not the contact list

Before adding names to a CRM, define why a productive agent or loan officer should take a conversation. Compensation may be part of the answer, but it is rarely the entire value proposition. A strong recruiting offer connects the professional to a business outcome: more qualified opportunities, better follow-up, operational leverage, stronger mentorship, or a clearer path to production.

Write the offer as a specific promise you can demonstrate. “We support our agents” is too broad. “We provide a documented lead follow-up system, a 30-day onboarding path, and a weekly production review” gives a candidate something concrete to evaluate. The same principle applies to mortgage teams: describe the referral, processing, marketing, or accountability system that helps a loan officer grow without carrying every task alone.

The three phases of a 90-day recruiting plan

Days 1–30: Position and prepare

The first month is for building the foundation. Choose one primary audience, such as experienced agents in a specific market, newly licensed agents who need structure, or loan officers who want more referral leverage. Then document the problems that audience is already trying to solve.

Turn those problems into a small recruiting content library. Publish a value proposition page, a practical guide, a producer-focused FAQ, and one proof point that shows how your operating model works. At the same time, configure the intake path: a landing page, a short form, CRM stages, permission-based follow-up, and a booking link. The goal is to make interest easy to capture and easy for a leader to understand.

Days 31–60: Create conversations

The second month is about consistent distribution and outreach. Share the content where your target professionals already pay attention, then connect it to a reason to respond. A guide about improving lead conversion can invite a confidential pipeline review. An onboarding checklist can invite a conversation about the first 30 days. A market-growth article can offer a simple planning session.

Use automation to deliver the promised resource, answer the next likely question, and remind the prospect about the conversation. Automation should create context, not impersonate the relationship. When a candidate replies or books, the recruiting leader should see their market, role, stated challenge, and content engagement before the meeting begins.

“The plan works when recruiting stops being a personality test for the owner. We can still bring judgment and relationship to the conversation, but the system should make sure the right people reach us consistently.”

— Brokerage owner perspective

Days 61–90: Qualify, onboard, and improve

The final phase is where a recruiting campaign becomes a growth system. Review which sources produce qualified conversations, which objections appear repeatedly, and where candidates stop moving forward. Then tighten the handoff between recruiting and onboarding. A signed agent should not enter a blank space; the first week, first month, technology setup, training, and support expectations should already be mapped.

This phase also protects quality. The goal is not to add names at any cost. It is to recruit professionals who fit the model and have a credible path to success inside it. A capacity-based target is healthier than a vanity target because it accounts for the leadership time and support required after the signature.

Use one scorecard to manage the whole funnel

Weekly review becomes much easier when the team tracks stages instead of disconnected activity. A brokerage can adapt the following scorecard to its market and capacity:

Stage Question to answer Management action
AttentionWhich topics and channels reach the right professionals?Keep the messages that attract the intended audience; remove generic reach.
InterestAre prospects raising their hand or returning for more information?Improve the offer, form, resource, or next step.
ConversationAre qualified prospects booking and attending?Clarify expectations and add context to reminders.
DecisionWhat concerns delay a move or a team change?Address proof, onboarding, economics, and fit transparently.
ActivationDo new agents reach their first milestones after signing?Strengthen onboarding and close the loop with recruiting.

What to automate and what to keep human

Automate the repetitive work: form notifications, CRM tagging, resource delivery, follow-up reminders, appointment confirmations, stage changes, and internal task creation. Keep the high-trust work human: explaining the model, discussing compensation, answering market-specific questions, qualifying mutual fit, and setting realistic expectations.

The practical rule: If a task must happen the same way every time, automate it. If it changes the candidate’s understanding of the relationship, let a leader own it.

Make the next 90 days easier than the last

A recruiting plan creates leverage when it leaves behind reusable assets: a clear producer offer, a content library, a CRM pipeline, a follow-up sequence, a recruiting scorecard, and a defined onboarding handoff. The next quarter should not begin with a blank page. It should begin with better evidence about which message, audience, and process create the strongest fit.

For brokerage owners and mortgage team leaders, consistent growth is less about finding one perfect recruiting tactic and more about connecting the layers. When positioning, attraction, follow-up, qualification, and onboarding reinforce each other, recruiting becomes a managed operating system instead of an occasional emergency.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What should a 90-day real estate recruiting plan include?

A 90-day real estate recruiting plan should include a clear producer offer, a defined target audience, weekly attraction and outreach activities, automated follow-up, scheduled recruiting conversations, and a scorecard for measuring progress from lead to signed agent.

How many agents should a brokerage try to recruit in 90 days?

The right target depends on market, capacity, and the brokerage's ability to onboard well. Set a capacity-based target by working backward from the number of agents the leadership team can personally qualify, support, and successfully onboard during the period.

Can recruiting follow-up be automated for a brokerage?

Yes. A brokerage can automate resource delivery, email and permission-based SMS follow-up, CRM stage changes, reminders, qualification questions, and booking links. Human leaders should still handle fit, expectations, compensation discussions, and the final relationship.
