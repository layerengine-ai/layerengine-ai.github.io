A2P 10DLC Explained: The SMS Compliance Recruiting Advantage in 2026

October 2, 2026 8 min read LayerEngine.ai

For a brokerage owner or mortgage team leader, recruiting SMS can feel simple: find the right people, send a message, and start conversations. But the message is only as valuable as the system behind it. Vague consent, mismatched campaign details, or an unclear opt-out path can make the channel unreliable before a real recruiting conversation begins.

A2P 10DLC is the U.S. application-to-person messaging framework for business texts sent through standard 10-digit long-code numbers. For recruiting leaders, it aligns the sender, message purpose, consent path, and recipient experience. Twilio describes it as a U.S. carrier standard designed to ensure long-code SMS traffic is verified and consensual. [1]

What A2P 10DLC means for recruiting leaders

A2P means application-to-person: a CRM, automation platform, or messaging tool sends a text to an individual. “10DLC” means the message uses a standard local-looking 10-digit number rather than a short code. If your brokerage automates recruiting follow-up through GoHighLevel, Twilio, or a similar platform, your program should be registered according to the platform’s applicable onboarding route and carrier requirements.

A personal text from a manager is not a scalable recruiting system. An application-driven program can capture interest, route the opportunity, remind the right person to follow up, and preserve the conversation record. But it also requires disciplined permissions: a phone number on a roster, referral list, or purchased database is not, by itself, permission to send automated marketing texts for your brokerage.

The three pieces of an approvable recruiting campaign

Reviewers should see one truthful story from your brand and landing page to the texts a prospect receives. HighLevel calls for alignment among the sender, use case, campaign description, sample messages, opt-in flow, website, and public policies. [2]

1. Register the business you actually operate

Start with accurate business information, a functional public website, and an identity that matches what prospective agents see. Do not register a vague campaign under one brand while sending messages from another name or landing page. This is especially important for a parent brokerage with multiple offices, teams, or recruiting brands. Decide which entity owns the program, then ensure the website, privacy policy, terms, and messaging signature support that identity.

2. Describe the recruiting use case without overpromising

A strong campaign description says who receives the texts, how they opted in, and what topics the program covers. “Recruit top agents” is not enough context. A more useful description might be: “Contacts who request our agent-growth guide may receive career resources, invitations to optional recruiting conversations, and appointment reminders from Northstar Realty Group.” Your sample messages should sound like the real communications your team intends to send—not generic templates that disappear after approval.

3. Make consent visible, optional, and provable

Your form should identify the brokerage, explain the message category, state message-frequency expectations, disclose that message and data rates may apply, and include HELP and STOP instructions. It should also link to accessible privacy and terms pages. HighLevel’s guidance says an SMS consent box should not be preselected or required to submit a form, and a phone number alone does not establish SMS permission. [2]

Build an SMS workflow that earns replies

Compliance does not mean sterile communication. It gives your team a clean foundation for communication that feels timely and relevant. Start with a useful reason to opt in: a market-specific agent guide, a compensation-model workshop, a recruiting event, or a request for a confidential career conversation. The consent language should match that offer.

After an opt-in, send a straightforward confirmation that identifies the brokerage and lets the prospect know what happens next. Then route the contact to a defined owner. Automation can acknowledge interest, schedule a reminder, and protect follow-up; a recruiter, branch manager, or team leader should own the human response. Keep the early sequence focused on value and choice, not repeated pressure. Every reply, STOP request, and escalation should have an unmistakable next action.

Compliance is a recruiting advantage when the system is visible

The same workflow that supports carrier review also improves leadership visibility. You can measure opt-ins, delivery status, replies, conversations, booked meetings, and opt-outs by campaign. Those stages reveal whether a recruiting problem is audience relevance, consent quality, message clarity, response speed, or the conversion conversation itself.

A 30-day A2P 10DLC recruiting launch plan

Week 1: Audit the current program

List every number, form, automation, and message currently used for agent or loan-officer recruiting. Identify the true sending brand, existing consent sources, policy pages, and the people who handle replies. Remove any workflow that cannot explain how the recipient gave permission.

Week 2: Build the consent-first campaign

Create one focused offer and landing page. Add optional SMS consent, accessible policy links, and clear confirmation, follow-up, STOP, and HELP behavior.

Week 3: Register and test the experience

Submit accurate business and campaign details through your messaging platform. Before launch, test the form, consent language, confirmation, booking step, routing, opt-out, and help response on real devices.

Week 4: Launch narrowly and review weekly

Begin with one audience and one campaign. Review opt-ins, replies, conversations, booked meetings, opt-outs, and delivery issues. Expand on evidence, not a desire to send more messages. This article is an operating framework, not legal advice; verify provider, carrier, and legal requirements before deployment.

The leadership takeaway: SMS compliance is not a box to check after your recruiting engine is built. It is the design constraint that helps you build a more credible agent experience, a cleaner automation workflow, and a recruiting pipeline your managers can actually measure.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does a real estate brokerage need A2P 10DLC for recruiting texts?

If a brokerage uses an application or CRM to send business SMS from a 10-digit long code in the United States, it should complete the applicable A2P 10DLC registration and align its campaign, consent flow, and messaging behavior with its provider and carrier requirements.

What should a real estate recruiting SMS opt-in include?

A recruiting opt-in should name the brokerage, explain the messages the recipient agrees to receive, include message-frequency and message-and-data-rate disclosures, provide HELP and STOP instructions, link to accessible privacy and terms pages, and use an optional, unchecked consent control.

Can a brokerage use purchased agent lists for SMS recruiting?

A phone number or a purchased contact record is not the same as documented consent for a brokerage’s SMS program. Before messaging, confirm the consent standard required by the sending platform, carrier rules, and applicable law, and maintain evidence of the recipient’s permission.
