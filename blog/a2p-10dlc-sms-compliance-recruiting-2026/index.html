A2P 10DLC Explained: The SMS Compliance Recruiting Advantage in 2026

November 16, 2026 8 min read LayerEngine.ai

SMS open rates are 98% — compared with 28% for email. Ninety percent of recipients open a text within three minutes. And 45% of recipients respond to branded messages, a rate 4.5 times higher than email. For brokerage owners and mortgage team leaders building a recruiting engine, those numbers represent a real channel advantage — but only when the foundation behind them is built correctly.

A2P 10DLC is the U.S. application-to-person messaging framework for business texts sent through standard 10-digit long-code numbers. 'A2P' means the message originates from a software platform rather than a person's phone. '10DLC' means it travels through a local-looking 10-digit number rather than a short code or toll-free number. Twilio describes A2P 10DLC as a U.S. carrier standard designed to ensure long-code SMS traffic is verified and consensual.

For a brokerage or mortgage team leader, A2P 10DLC registration is not a bureaucratic checkpoint. It is the mechanism that aligns the sender identity, message purpose, consent path, and recipient experience into one consistent story — and it is what separates a durable recruiting pipeline from a program that works until it does not.

What A2P 10DLC Means for Recruiting Leaders

A personal text from a manager is not a scalable recruiting system. An application-driven program can capture interest, route the opportunity, remind the right person to follow up, and preserve the conversation record. But it also requires disciplined permissions: a phone number on a roster, referral list, or purchased database is not, by itself, permission to send automated marketing texts for your brokerage.

Research from Learning Hub found that only 13% of organizations let consumers respond to promotional SMS, yet 60% of recipients want to be able to reply. For a passive recruiting funnel built on inbound interest, the ability to respond meaningfully — and the confidence that responses will reach the right person — is a measurable competitive advantage.

Registration does not guarantee delivery. It establishes the sender's identity and campaign purpose with carriers, which reduces the filtering and blocking that erodes throughput as an unregistered program scales. The difference between registered and unregistered traffic shows up in the campaigns where volume matters most.

The Three Pieces of an Approvable Recruiting Campaign

Reviewers look for one truthful story from your brand and landing page to the texts a prospect actually receives. HighLevel's compliance guidance calls for alignment among the sender, use case, campaign description, sample messages, opt-in flow, website, and public policies. A gap between any two of those elements is a reason to reject or delay a campaign.

1. Register the business you actually operate

Start with accurate business information, a functional public website, and an identity that matches what prospective agents see. Do not register a vague campaign under one brand while sending messages from another name or landing page. This is especially important for a parent brokerage with multiple offices, teams, or recruiting brands. Decide which entity owns the program — then make sure the website, privacy policy, terms, and messaging signature all support that identity. A phone number registered to one brand and a landing page under a different brand name is a common reason campaigns are rejected without any content problem at all.

2. Describe the recruiting use case without overpromising

A strong campaign description says who receives the texts, how they opted in, and what topics the program covers. 'Recruit top agents' is not enough context. A clearer version looks like this: 'Contacts who request our agent-growth guide may receive career resources, invitations to optional recruiting conversations, and appointment reminders from Northstar Realty Group.'

Sample messages in a campaign registration should look like the real communications your team intends to send. Generic placeholders that disappear after approval create inconsistency between the registered use case and the live program — and that inconsistency can become a compliance problem after launch, not just before.

3. Make consent visible, optional, and provable

HighLevel's compliance guidance is specific: an SMS consent checkbox should not be preselected or required to submit a form, and a phone number alone does not establish SMS permission. The form should identify the brokerage, explain the message category, state message-frequency expectations, disclose that message and data rates may apply, and include HELP and STOP instructions with links to accessible privacy and terms pages.

The requirement for documented, optional consent is not only a compliance standard. It is a signal to the prospect that the brokerage treats their information and attention with care. That signal matters most in the early recruiting relationship — where trust is being formed before a single conversation has happened.

Build an SMS Workflow That Earns Replies

Compliance does not mean sterile communication. A registered program gives your team a clean foundation for messages that feel timely and relevant rather than uncertain. Start with a genuine reason to opt in: a market-specific agent growth guide, a compensation-model comparison, a recruiting event, or a request for a confidential career conversation. The consent language should match that offer exactly.

After an opt-in, send a straightforward confirmation that identifies the brokerage and tells the prospect what happens next. Then route the contact to a defined owner. Automation can acknowledge interest, schedule a reminder, and protect follow-up timing; a recruiter, branch manager, or team leader should own the human response. Keep early sequences focused on value and choice. Every reply, STOP request, and escalation should have a defined next action — not an assumption that someone will notice.

The same workflow that supports carrier review also creates brokerage-wide recruiting visibility. Opt-ins, delivery status, replies, conversations, booked meetings, and opt-outs by campaign show exactly where a recruiting program gains or loses momentum. Those stages reveal whether the problem is audience relevance, consent quality, message clarity, response speed, or the conversion conversation itself.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Does a real estate brokerage need A2P 10DLC for recruiting texts?

If your brokerage uses a CRM or automation platform — such as GoHighLevel — to send business SMS from a 10-digit long-code number in the United States, it should complete the applicable A2P 10DLC registration and align its campaign, consent flow, and messaging with provider and carrier requirements. Unregistered programs can face filtering at scale and provide no documented compliance record if a carrier question arises.

What should a real estate recruiting SMS opt-in include?

A compliant recruiting opt-in should name the brokerage, explain the messages the recipient agrees to receive, include message-frequency and message-and-data-rate disclosures, provide HELP and STOP instructions, link to accessible privacy and terms pages, and use an optional, unchecked consent control. Combining required information into a single checkbox or pre-filling the field are the two most common reasons a form fails compliance review.

Can a brokerage use purchased agent lists for SMS recruiting?

A phone number or purchased contact record is not the same as documented consent for a specific brokerage SMS program. Before messaging a purchased list, confirm the consent standard required by your sending platform, carrier rules, and applicable law. If the consent source cannot be described in a campaign registration — who collected it, when, under what offer — the list is not ready for automated SMS outreach. The passive recruiting funnel approach — earning inbound interest through branded content and clear value — produces better compliance documentation and better conversion rates than outbound list-blasting.

How does A2P 10DLC registration affect message deliverability?

Registration establishes the sender's identity and campaign purpose with U.S. carriers, which reduces the filtering and blocking that grows more common as unregistered programs increase their sending volume. It does not guarantee delivery, but it removes one of the primary structural reasons messages fail to reach recipients at scale. The throughput difference between registered and unregistered programs tends to become visible at the exact moment a recruiting campaign is performing well and the team wants to send more.

SMS compliance is a recruiting advantage — not a constraint.
